> The beginning of my journey into cybersecurity, AI security research, and software engineering documenting what I learn, build, question, and get wrong along the way.

## This is where I start.

I'm a Computer Science student at Rutgers University, exploring cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, and software engineering.

I have a long-term goal of becoming an AI security engineer and researcher. But having a goal and knowing how to get there are two very different things.

There are concepts I understand, concepts I've forgotten, and plenty I haven't learned yet. Sometimes I can follow an explanation but struggle to implement it myself. Sometimes something that looks simple turns into hours of debugging.

I don't want to hide that part of the process.

That's why I'm building this website.

## Why document the journey?

Most portfolios show completed projects, certifications, polished screenshots, and final results.

There's nothing wrong with that. But I've always been curious about what happens before those results exist.

How did someone learn to build their first security tool? What did their first research experiment look like? What mistakes did they make? How did they move from reading about a technology to actually understanding and building something with it?

I want to document those questions through my own experiences.

Not because I have everything figured out, but because I want to be able to look back and see how my thinking developed.

## What I am working toward

My interests currently revolve around three connected fields.

Cybersecurity. I want to understand how systems are attacked, how vulnerabilities emerge, and how defenders can detect and prevent malicious behavior.

Artificial intelligence. I'm interested in how machine learning models work, how they learn patterns, and what makes them reliable or vulnerable.

Software engineering. I want to become capable of building useful systems rather than only understanding their theory.

Eventually, I want these interests to come together in AI security research.

But I'm approaching that ambition one concept, one experiment, and one project at a time.

## What you will find here

This website will be a record of what I actually do.

I'll share notes from research papers, cybersecurity labs, programming projects, experiments, and technical problems I encounter.

Some entries might explain a concept I've just learned. Others might document a project that failed, a bug that took too long to fix, or an idea I haven't fully developed.

When I conduct an experiment, I want to explain what I expected to happen, what actually happened, and what I learned from the difference.

I also want to revisit older entries as my understanding improves.

The goal isn't to make every post impressive. It's to make every post useful or meaningful.

## What I don't know yet

One thing I want to be clear about: I'm still learning.

I want to strengthen my programming fundamentals, deepen my understanding of computer security, learn how modern AI systems are built, and eventually conduct original research.

That's a significant amount of work.

I expect to misunderstand things, make incorrect assumptions, write code that doesn't work, and spend time investigating problems that turn out to have simple explanations.

When that happens, I want to document the process rather than quietly erase it.

## A note to my future self

If I'm reading this years from now, I hope I can recognize how much has changed.

I hope the projects are more sophisticated, the research questions are more challenging, and the things that confuse me today have become foundations for understanding something deeper.

But I also hope I haven't forgotten what it felt like to begin.

This is the starting point.

** No perfect roadmap. No pretending to know everything. Just learning, building, questioning, and getting better. **

— Divy Brahmbhatt
